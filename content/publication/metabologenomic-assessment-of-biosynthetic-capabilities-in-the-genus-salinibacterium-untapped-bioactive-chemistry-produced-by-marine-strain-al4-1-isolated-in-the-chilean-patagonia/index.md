abstract: Antimicrobial resistance represents one of the most critical global
  public health challenges. Increasing evidence suggests that rare actinomycetes
  can harbour novel biosynthetic diversity to produce antimicrobial compounds.
  This work explores the capabilities for the biosynthesis of antimicrobial
  natural products of Salinibacterium sp. AL4.1, in contrast with those of
  Salinibacterium sp. VpJ6, through a comparative metabologenomic approach.
  Comparative bioactivity tests using both Salinibacterium strains AL4.1 and
  VpJ6 revealed antimicrobial bioactivity of crude extracts from strain AL4.1
  against Staphylococcus aureus. Liquid chromatography coupled with tandem mass
  spectrometry was performed on the tested samples, allowing for metabolite
  dereplication as well as identification of bioactivity-associated features.
  This revealed the presence of potential aromatic polyketides structurally
  related to known bioactive compounds such as phloretin, for which bacterial
  production had not been reported to date, as well as other potentially novel
  alkaloids and peptides. The genomes of both strains were sequenced and mined
  for their biosynthetic capabilities and further compared to those of other
  Salinibacterium strains with available genomic data. Phylogenomic studies
  suggested that both strains AL4.1 and VpJ6 could represent potentially new
  species. A mainly phylogeny-dependent distribution of biosynthetic gene
  clusters (BGCs) was observed, emphasizing the presence of a widely distributed
  type III polyketide synthase BGC, which could be linked to the bioactive
  compounds. These results suggest that Salinibacterium sp. AL4.1 can produce
  novel bioactive natural products, and the genus Salinibacterium holds relevant
  and underexplored biosynthetic potential.
